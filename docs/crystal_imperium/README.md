# Pokémon Crystal Imperium

The Johto and Kanto adventure of **Pokémon Heart & Soul** (a Gen 2 remake on the
pokeemerald-expansion GBA engine), rebuilt around the team building, quality of life and
competitive difficulty of **Pokémon Emerald Imperium**, with every Gen 1–9 Pokémon catchable.

> **Status: in development (0.15.1-experimental).** The full Heart & Soul story is in and
> playable. All the systems listed below work and are tested in an emulator. Every trainer
> uses the smart AI and fights at your level, every boss from the rival to Red, rematches and
> postgame included, has an Imperium-style team with all of its Pokémon at the level cap, Ace
> Trainers wait on the routes, the Pokémon have Emerald Imperium's abilities, move pools and
> evolutions (from its official dex), the TM list is Emerald Imperium's, and HMs work without
> being taught. Nobody has played the whole story through yet.

## What you get

From the first step of a new game:

- **Key items:** Rare Candy Box (unlimited Rare Candies up to the level cap), Infinite Repel (an
  on/off switch), Pokémon Box Link (the PC anywhere), Training Kit (change nature, ability, IVs
  and EVs any time), PokéVial (full heal outside battle, never runs out).
- **The L menu**, as in Emerald Imperium: press **L** in the field for the PokéVial, a PC (all
  of a Pokémon Center's), the DexNav, **TIME** (make it morning, day, evening or night: the
  clock moves forward to that time and the map follows at once), **STATS** (the Training Kit:
  nature, ability, and an IV and EV editor) and the **REPEL**, **FOLLOWER** and **AUTO RUN**
  switches (green when on), with the clock at the top right. Nothing needs the bag. (Not with
  BUTTON MODE L=A, where L is the A button.)
- **IV and EV editor** (L > STATS > IVs & EVs): each stat's IV, EV and value, Hidden Power's
  type and the EVs used; A changes a value (UP/DOWN by 1, LEFT/RIGHT by 10), START sets every
  IV to 31. EVs stop at 252 a stat and 510 in all. With Minimal Grinding on, the EVs are locked
  and the IVs only set Hidden Power's type, as in Emerald Imperium.
- **Quick Rare Candies:** choose a Pokémon and how many to use (at first, all of them up to the
  level cap); they are used at once, with one message, no stat pages, and no level-up jingle,
  so the music keeps playing. Moves of every level passed are still learned.
- **Fast Egg hatching:** an Egg cycle is 8 steps instead of 256, so most Eggs hatch in a
  short walk (a Togepi Egg in about 90 steps).
- **DexNav** (L menu):
  - It shows each area's Pokémon by icon and name, seen or not: the area's own, its Wild
    Variety Pokémon and three hidden ones. The list is the same at every hour and every day:
    an area's morning, day and night Pokémon are all there, and all of them turn up at any
    time. What you run into in the grass or on the water is on the list (with Heart & Soul's
    randomizer on, the randomized Pokémon are). Pick any of them: A searches, R registers.
  - SELECT lists **every** Gen 1–9 Pokémon (all 1,074: every evolution stage, regional
    forms, Legendaries), and you can search for any of them anywhere. LEFT/RIGHT jump 100.
  - Walk or run up to a Pokémon found by searching to battle it: no sneaking needed. It stays
    where it appeared, in caves and on water too. In Dark Cave, Flash lights the whole cave, as
    in Gold, Silver and Crystal, and DexNav works there.
  - R repeats a search for the registered Pokémon; L (or START) ends a search and keeps the
    DexNav chain.
  - Hidden Pokémon are also detected while walking.
  - Search levels improve the chances of egg moves, Hidden Abilities and held items.
- **Wild Variety:** every area has its own set of other Gen 1–9 Pokémon (up to six in the
  grass and three Water-types on the water, as many as fit in the DexNav row beside the area's
  own, already evolved if the area's level allows it), shown in DexNav. 30% of wild encounters
  (adjustable) are one of them. An area keeps its set (it changed every day before 0.14.1).
- **Easy Catch:** every Poké Ball catches (can be turned off).
- **Faster battles** (START > OPTION > BATTLE: FAST BATTLES and FAST INTRO, both on): no
  slide-in at the start; the intro, move animations, the Pokémon's own animations, the HP bar
  and trainers sliding in at double speed; a shorter hit flash; and messages such as "fainted!"
  or "gained Exp. Points!" go on by themselves after a moment (A or B at once; a trainer's
  words wait for you). A chosen move starts at once: "X used Y!" appears whole and the animation
  follows a quarter of a second after you pick the move (about three times sooner). The battle no
  longer pauses on its own bookkeeping or waits for a cry to end. A trainer battle against one
  Pokémon takes about a third less time.
- **Minimal Grinding Mode** (every Pokémon battles as if it had 31 IVs and 0 EVs), **level caps**,
  ×1.5 EXP, an **EASY / NORMAL / HARD** difficulty selection, **Auto Run**
  (START > OPTION > AUTORUN, or the L menu), and a **move relearner** in the party menu.
- **Emerald Imperium-style level caps:** 15 before the first Gym, then 24, 33, 42, 50, 58, 66,
  75 and **85 for the Elite Four**, 90 in Kanto, and no cap after the Kanto League (HARD caps: a
  few levels lower until the Elite Four).
- **Every trainer fights at your level**, as in Emerald Imperium: an ordinary trainer's Pokémon
  are one to three levels below your strongest (never below their old place on the cap curve,
  never above the cap), evolved as far as their level allows (no level-72 Dragonair), with 31 IVs.
- **Six Pokémon for every Gym Leader** and the big story bosses, and **28 route mini-bosses**:
  strong trainers on the routes with competitive teams at your level cap, as in Emerald
  Imperium.
- **Ace Trainers on the routes:** 59 of them, as in Emerald Imperium, where they see you on the
  routes' main paths (26 in Johto, 33 in Kanto), with teams built around a plan (sand, rain,
  sun, snow, Trick Room, hazards, screens): three Pokémon on the first routes, six with
  competitive EVs in Kanto. Heart & Soul's own Cooltrainers have competitive teams too.
- **The level cap is the level of every boss, and nothing is above it:** bosses are at your cap
  (NORMAL or HARD caps, on every difficulty), and no trainer's, wild, DexNav, legendary or gift
  Pokémon is above it.
- **HARD plays by Emerald Imperium's and Radical Red's rules:** the SET battle style (no "Will
  you switch Pokémon?" after a knockout) and no Bag in trainer battles, on top of +10% trainer
  levels and HARD level caps.
- **Emerald Imperium's abilities and move pools:** every Pokémon has Imperium's abilities,
  level-up moves, Egg Moves, and TM and tutor moves, from its official dex, so Pidgey has No
  Guard, Golbat Infiltrator and Typhlosion Flash Fire, Typhlosion learns Aura Sphere and Mystical
  Fire, Pikachu Zippy Zap at 55 and Primeape Shadow Punch at 35. Imperium's postgame TMs Draco
  Barrage and Dark Hole are in, as tutor moves after the Hall of Fame. Imperium's own abilities
  (Striker, Bone Zone...) aren't in this engine.
- **A Gen 5 look:** Black/White-style battles (healthboxes, text box, FIGHT / BAG / POKÉMON /
  RUN buttons, a move box with types, PP and effectiveness) and a Black/White-style summary
  screen (A on the skills page shows IVs and EVs).
- **Emerald Imperium's TMs:** 198 reusable TMs. TM01–TM172 are Imperium's list in its order
  (without its two custom moves), TM173–TM198 the Heart & Soul TMs Imperium doesn't have.
  **The care packages bring them, free:** the starting package the 71 with status moves and
  moves under 70 power, then each Johto badge's package the next ones (44 with the first, 40
  with the second, 18 with the third: Ice Beam, Thunderbolt, Flamethrower...), all 198 by the
  eighth. Every Poké Mart still has a TM clerk (¥100 a TM, Imperium's price) whose stock grows
  with every badge in the same steps, for anything you're missing or, with INFINITE TMS off,
  for more copies; its list leaves out the TMs you already have.
- **Emerald Imperium's evolutions**, from its official dex: trade evolutions also take a Linking
  Cord, regional forms come by time of day or an item anywhere (a Shiny Stone makes Alolan Raichu,
  Quilava at 36 at night becomes Hisuian Typhlosion), friendship and special methods became
  levels or stones (Munchlax at 30, Kingambit at 50, Gliscor with a Dusk Stone, Annihilape from a
  Primeape that knows Shadow Punch), and many Pokémon evolve earlier (starters at 16 and 36,
  Pidgey and Rattata at 16). [EVOLUTIONS.md](EVOLUTIONS.md) lists how every Pokémon evolves.
  The Goldenrod Dept. Store 2F sells Imperium's fifteen evolution items for ¥500 each (every
  stone, the Linking Cord, Prism Scale, King's Rock, Metal Coat and Up-Grade) and the Berries that
  raise friendship.
- **HMs without teaching:** carrying the HM (and its badge) lets any Pokémon in the party use
  Cut, Surf, Strength, Rock Smash, Waterfall, and Fly or Flash from the lead's party menu.
- **A starting care package** from Prof. Elm as soon as you have a Pokémon, as in Emerald
  Imperium: the Shiny Charm, 153 Quick Balls, 10 of every type-boosting item and every type-resist
  Berry, Leftovers, Focus Sashes, Eviolites and more. **Nine location packages** wait along the
  story, each with a theme (the weather Rocks in Union Cave, the Choice items in the National
  Park, the Terrain Seeds at the Lake of Rage, ...).
- **Care packages** from Prof. Elm after every badge and after becoming Champion, Emerald
  Imperium's (that is, Radical Red's): 10 of nearly every held item, Berry, booster and evolution
  item, and big stacks (50 Heal Balls after the first badge, 153 Ability Capsules and 153
  Ability Patches after the second, 50 Dream Balls and the Choice items after the third, 153 PP
  Max after the sixth, 100 Beast Balls for the Champion), and the TMs (above). Each package
  lists what's inside; if a Bag pocket is full, the rest goes to your PC, and if the PC is full
  too, the package waits.
- **Mega Evolution** (47 classic Megas plus Rayquaza; stones from Goldenrod Dept. Store 5F, or
  all at once with the MEGA code).
- **Emerald Imperium's cheat codes** at the NES in your room: turn it on, answer YES to "Enter a
  code?" and type one (any case):

  | Code | Alias | Does |
  |---|---|---|
  | MEGA | | the Mega Ring and every Mega Stone (47); enter it again for any that didn't fit |
  | MAXMONEY | MMY | money to the maximum |
  | CAUGHTALL | CAL | every Pokémon caught in the National Pokédex |
  | EZCATCH | EZC | switches Easy Catch off or on |
  | SHINYS | SHS | PROF. ELM's Pokémon shiny (before you choose one) |
  | MONO | | pick a type for PROF. ELM's three Pokémon (before you choose one) |
  | SHINYR | SHR | the roaming Pokémon shiny |
  | DEXALL | DXA | the DexNav already shows every Pokémon |
- Legendary and Mythical Pokémon are on the DexNav list from the start. Once you are
  Champion, about a quarter of the areas' Wild Variety sets include one.
- **Cheat menu:** hold R and press START in the overworld for HnS's debug menu (give any
  Pokémon or item, and more; *Flags & Vars… → Pokédex Flags All* fills the Pokédex). Use it
  with care: it can also change story flags.
- **Smart AI for every trainer** on NORMAL and HARD: it knows your team, picks the best move,
  switches smartly and plays around HP (EASY keeps Heart & Soul's AI). Sleep Clause.
- **Imperium-style bosses for the whole story and the postgame:** every Johto Gym Leader
  (Chuck, Jasmine and Pryce in three versions, for whichever order you take them), the rival
  from Azalea to the Indigo Plateau, Team Rocket's executives, the Kimono Girls, the Elite Four
  and Lance, the eight Kanto leaders, Blue and Red, and every rematch: the Johto leaders in the
  Fighting Dojo, the Elite Four again after the Johto League, and all of them after the Kanto
  League, with legendaries and Paradox Pokémon. **Every boss Pokémon is at your level cap** (not just the ace;
  with HARD level caps, the HARD cap), with held items, natures, abilities, competitive moves,
  and Mega Evolutions from the 5th Gym on. A boss you fight later than usual (the Rocket Hideout
  with six badges, a rematch after the Kanto League) is raised to your cap then; rematches after
  the Kanto League are at 100.
- **Red is the hardest battle in the game:** all six at level 100, competitive EVs that only his
  Pokémon keep under Minimal Grinding, the boss AI with move and switch prediction, four Full
  Restores, and a sun team around Mega Charizard Y. He battles only the Kanto Champion, so win
  the Kanto League first (the level cap is gone by then).
- **Side battles, Emerald Imperium-style:** Gym Leaders from newer regions wait around the map.
  Talk to one, answer YES, and win for a reward, once: ten Gems of their type and items such as
  TMs and Mega Stones before the shops sell them, the Rotom Catalog, the Exp. Charm and the
  Catching Charm. Sinnoh's eight in Johto, one for each badge count (Candice in Violet City's
  Trainer School from the start); Paldea's eight in Kanto after the Johto League; and after the
  Kanto League, nine visitors at level 100 on the way to Red: Cynthia, Iris, Geeta, N, Cyrus,
  Wallace, Maxie, Archie and Wally. Each has their own sprites (Credits).

All of these are in the challenge menu at the start of a new game (and on the settings PC in
your house), except Auto Run, which is in the Options menu and the L menu.

## Building

You do **not** need a Pokémon ROM; the game is compiled entirely from source.

On Ubuntu 24.04 / Debian (or WSL2 on Windows):

```sh
sudo apt install build-essential git libpng-dev gcc-arm-none-eabi binutils-arm-none-eabi libnewlib-arm-none-eabi
# optional, for the emulator play tests:
sudo apt install libmgba-dev
git clone https://github.com/Cvega6950/glowing-octo-potato.git
cd glowing-octo-potato
git checkout claude/pokemon-crystal-imperium-vsmmzc
tools/crystal_imperium/build.sh          # -> CrystalImperium.gba
tools/crystal_imperium/build.sh --test   # also run the emulator play tests
```

The first build takes a few minutes. Tested tool chain: arm-none-eabi-gcc 13.2.1, libmgba 0.10.2.

## Patches

`tools/crystal_imperium/make_patches.sh` writes both patches into `build/release/`:

- **Source patch series** (`git format-patch` on top of Heart & Soul's tag `2.0.6`): apply it
  to an HnS 2.0.6 checkout with `git am` and build as above.
- **BPS patch** from the Heart & Soul 2.0.6 ROM, built from that tag with the same tool chain,
  to `CrystalImperium.gba`. Apply it with Flips or Rom Patcher JS. `SHA1SUMS` lists both ROMs'
  hashes; the patch only applies to that exact HnS ROM. The script checks that the patch
  rebuilds the ROM byte for byte.

No patch against an original Nintendo ROM is provided, and no ROM files are distributed from
this repository.

## Playing on Delta (iPhone)

1. Get `CrystalImperium.gba` onto the phone. Either:
   - build it as above and copy it over (AirDrop, iCloud Drive or Files), or
   - make this GitHub repository **private**, then open *Actions → Crystal Imperium → latest
     run → Artifacts* and download `CrystalImperium-<commit>` on the phone. CI only uploads
     the ROM for private repositories, because the ROM contains Nintendo's copyrighted
     assets.
2. In Delta, tap **+** → **Files** and pick `CrystalImperium.gba`.
3. Use the in-game SAVE (START → SAVE). Delta keeps the cartridge save between sessions.
4. If the day/night clock misbehaves, open the settings PC in your house and set Clock Type
   to the ROM's fake clock.

Device testing on Delta has not been done yet. Please report what happens using the checklist
in [TESTING.md](TESTING.md).

## Saves

Saves from Heart & Soul 2.0.6 and from earlier Crystal Imperium builds load. On Continue they
receive any key items they are missing and the DexNav (so their L menu is complete), and their TMs keep their moves under the
new numbering (Heart & Soul's TM70 Rock Climb becomes TM192, for example). Easy Catch and Wild Variety (30%) are on
for them, and any care packages for badges already earned arrive one per step. Minimal
Grinding starts OFF for HnS saves and can be turned on from the settings PC.
